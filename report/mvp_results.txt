--- Honest finding: PSNR and SSIM disagree on which method is better ---

PSNR: learned refinement wins decisively at both sparsity levels (+11.8 dB at 16 sensors,
+10.6 dB at 64 sensors).

SSIM: time-reversal wins at both sparsity levels (0.71/0.73 vs 0.50/0.52 for learned).

Diagnosed (example 0, see report/mvp_ssim_diagnosis.png), not left unexplained:
- Within the actual structure (ground truth > 0.05): learned-model SSIM = 0.936 vs
  time-reversal SSIM = 0.188 -- the learned model is dramatically, unambiguously better where
  the signal actually is, consistent with the qualitative comparison in mvp_comparison.png.
- In the background (>85% of image area, ground truth approx 0): time-reversal SSIM = 0.870 vs
  learned-model SSIM = 0.405. Cause: the U-Net (trained with plain per-pixel MSE loss) leaves a
  faint residual "haze" across the background -- corner-region mean 0.0099 / std 0.0039, versus
  ground truth's exact 0 and time-reversal's near-zero 0.0004 / std 0.0006. This haze is small in
  absolute magnitude (which is why PSNR, a global per-pixel metric, barely penalises it) but
  SSIM's local-variance sensitivity penalises it heavily, and background pixels dominate the
  image by area, so it dominates the whole-image SSIM average.

Conclusion: the learned model is genuinely and substantially better at the actual reconstruction
task (recovering source structure, removing streak artefacts), but introduces a systematic
low-level background bias that a pure-MSE loss does not penalise enough to suppress. This is a
real, known failure mode of MSE-trained image-restoration networks, not a bug in the evaluation
code (verified via the per-pixel SSIM map and the region-masked comparison above). Not fixed in
the MVP -- a plausible future direction (not implemented) would be an additional background/
sparsity-promoting loss term, but that is strong-version/extension scope, not MVP scope.
